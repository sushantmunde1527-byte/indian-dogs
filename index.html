History Of Dogs In India

The history of dogs in India is long, diverse, and closely tied to human civilization, culture, and daily life. Dogs have been present in the Indian subcontinent since ancient times. Archaeological evidence from the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) shows that dogs were domesticated and lived alongside humans. Seals and figurines suggest they were used for guarding homes and assisting in hunting. Some burials even indicate that dogs were valued companions. In the Rigveda, one of the oldest texts in the world, dogs are mentioned in symbolic and spiritual contexts. The deity associated with death, Yama, is described as having two four-eyed dogs who guard the path to the afterlife.

During the period of kingdoms and empires, dogs were widely used for hunting and protection. Royal families, including rulers like Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, are often linked with loyal dogs in folklore.

Waghya Dog Story

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj is often associated with a loyal dog named Waghya in popular stories and folklore.

Waghya is said to have been a devoted companion of Shivaji Maharaj. According to legend, after Shivaji Maharaj’s death in 1680, Waghya jumped onto his funeral pyre out of loyalty. A statue of Waghya exists near the samadhi (memorial) of Shivaji Maharaj at Raigad Fort.

However, it is important to note that there is no strong historical evidence confirming Waghya’s existence. Many historians consider this story to be folklore rather than proven history.

Indian Pariah Dog

Indian Pariah Dog

Home

Scientific Name : Canis lupus familiaris

The Indian Pariah Dog is one of the oldest dog types in the world. It is a natural breed that evolved without human-controlled breeding.

They are very intelligent, quick learners, and have strong memory. They are loyal, protective, and form strong emotional bonds with owners.

This breed is perfectly suited for Indian climate. It tolerates heat, rain, and is resistant to many diseases.

They require simple food such as rice, vegetables, eggs, and meat occasionally. Grooming is very low as they have short coat.

They are excellent guard dogs and can sense danger quickly. With proper care, they become one of the best pets for Indian homes.

Rajapalayam Dog

Rajapalayam Dog

Home

Scientific Name : Canis lupus familiaris

Rajapalayam is a rare Indian dog breed originating from Tamil Nadu India. This breed is known for its white coat pink nose and strong body. Rajapalayam dogs were historically used for hunting wild boar and guarding palaces.

Rajapalayam dogs are very loyal and protective. They are excellent guard dogs and fearless in nature. This breed requires proper training and exercise. They are very devoted to family but may be aggressive towards strangers.

The Rajapalayam Dog is a rare and ancient breed native to the town of Rajapalayam in southern India. Known for its striking appearance and strong guarding instincts, this breed has historically been associated with royalty and warfare. During the time of the Carnatic Wars, Rajapalayam dogs were reportedly used by local rulers as war dogs to attack enemy cavalry.

Physically, the Rajapalayam is a large, muscular sighthound with a deep chest and long legs built for speed and endurance. One of its most distinctive features is its pure white coat, which is short, dense, and easy to maintain. The breed typically has a pink nose, golden or light-colored eyes, and a whip-like tail. Adult dogs usually stand between 65 to 75 cm tall and can weigh around 25 to 32 kilograms.

In terms of temperament, Rajapalayam dogs are known for being loyal, intelligent, and highly protective. They tend to form a strong bond with one primary owner and can be wary or even aggressive toward strangers if not properly socialized. Early training and consistent discipline are essential.

The breed thrives best in spacious environments such as farms or large homes with yards. Grooming needs are minimal, but attention should be given to their diet and health.

Overall, the Rajapalayam dog is powerful, loyal, and dignified. It is one of the best guard dogs in India and an important part of Indian heritage.

Mudhol Hound

Mudhol Hound

Home

Scientific Name : Canis lupus familiaris

Mudhol Hound also known as Caravan Hound originates from Karnataka India. These dogs are fast agile and excellent hunters. Mudhol hounds have slim body long legs and short coat.

They require regular exercise and open space. Mudhol dogs are loyal and intelligent. They are used by Indian army and police forces.

The Mudhol Hound is a sighthound, meaning it hunts primarily by sight rather than scent. It has a lean body with long legs and a deep chest, allowing it to run at high speed.

The coat is short and smooth and comes in various colors such as fawn, black, cream, or white. Adult dogs usually stand between 68 to 74 cm tall.

In terms of temperament, the Mudhol Hound is loyal and intelligent but somewhat reserved. It bonds strongly with its owner but may be cautious with strangers.

This breed requires open space and is not suitable for apartments. It is energetic and needs regular exercise to stay healthy.

Mudhol Hounds are well adapted to Indian climate and have fewer health problems compared to foreign breeds.

Kombai Dog

Home

Scientific Name : Canis lupus familiaris

Kombai dog originates from Tamil Nadu India. This breed is known for strength bravery and loyalty. Kombai dogs were used for hunting and guarding.

They are muscular strong and fearless. Kombai dogs are protective and intelligent. They require training and exercise.

The Kombai Dog is a powerful and ancient native breed from the town of Kombai. Known for its courage, loyalty, and strong guarding instincts, it is one of India’s most formidable indigenous breeds.

Historically, it was used by warriors and rulers for protection and hunting in forest regions. It has a strong build with a muscular body and a short coat.

In terms of temperament, the Kombai is extremely loyal and protective of its family. It is fearless and will defend its territory. However, it can be aggressive toward strangers if not properly trained.

Training requires patience and experience. Early socialization is very important to control aggressive behavior.

The breed needs open space and regular exercise. It is not suitable for apartment living.

Overall, the Kombai dog is brave, loyal, and an excellent guard dog suited for experienced owners.

Chippiparai is a fast Indian dog breed from Tamil Nadu. These dogs are slim fast and intelligent. They were used for hunting.

Gaddi Kutta

Gaddi Kutta

Home

Scientific Name : Canis lupus familiaris

Gaddi Kutta is a mountain dog from Himachal Pradesh India. These dogs are strong and used for guarding livestock.

They are intelligent loyal and protective. Gaddi dogs adapt to cold weather.

The Gaddi Kutta is a strong working breed used by shepherds to protect livestock from predators like leopards and wolves.

It has a large muscular body and a thick coat which protects it from cold climate. It is very brave and fearless.

In terms of temperament, it is highly protective and loyal. It forms strong bonds with owners but may be aggressive toward strangers.

Training should be done early and handled by experienced owners.

This breed needs large open space and is not suitable for city apartments.

Overall, Gaddi Kutta is a powerful, loyal, and hardworking dog suitable for rural and mountain areas.
